Three screw families, kept apart because they use different diameters, threads and holes.

The machine screw chart covers #0 to 1 inch in UNC and UNF threads. The metric chart covers M1.6 to M24. The wood screw chart covers #2 to #14 and gives pilot holes.

Choosing the right chart

Start with how the screw is labelled. A letter M followed by a number means metric. A # sign, or a fraction such as 1/4, means an inch size. If the screw has a sharp tapered point and a deep thread, it is almost certainly a wood screw. The explorer above searches one family at a time because the same number means different things in each: #8 is 0.164 in for both machine and wood screws, but the thread, the tap drill and the clearance hole all differ.

If you only have the screw in your hand, measure the outside of the thread with a caliper and use Match my screw on the drill bit page. Measure the thread crests, not the head, and expect a small error.
